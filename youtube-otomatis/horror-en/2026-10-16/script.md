## FULL NARRATION SCRIPT (winning hook = opening)

### beat-001
More than 12,000 strangers have reported the exact same shadow figure.
They live on different continents. They speak different languages. They have never met.
Every single one describes a tall man wearing a wide-brimmed hat.
Not a demon with horns. Not a ghost in white. A man. With a hat.
The reports come from forums, sleep clinics, and late-night radio callers.
They span 40 years. They span the entire planet.
And the description never changes.
**[ON SCREEN: montage of anonymous testimonial quotes, map pins lighting up across continents]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Dark cinematic world map photographed on aged paper, three small red pins glowing in different continents, a tall silhouette of a man with a wide-brimmed hat and long coat rising behind the map, heavy film grain, practical lighting, no text, 16:9

### beat-002
They call him the Hat Man.
He shows up in bedrooms at night. He stands in doorways. He watches people sleep.
Sometimes he leans against a wall. Sometimes he sits at the foot of the bed.
And here is what makes him different from every other ghost story ever told.
Most ghosts haunt one house. This one haunts the entire human species.
He does not belong to one family, one town, or one legend.
He belongs to everyone, everywhere, all at once.
**[ON SCREEN: the name "THE HAT MAN" forms from darkness, then a doorway with a tall silhouette]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Extremely dark bedroom interior at night, bedroom doorway framing a tall motionless silhouette wearing a wide-brimmed hat and long coat, faint moonlight from a side window, subtle film grain, practical horror photography, no text, 16:9

### beat-003
Thousands of people describe him the same way.
6 feet tall or more. Black from head to toe, darker than the darkness around him.
A wide-brimmed hat, like something from the 1800s. A long coat that reaches his knees.
He never speaks. He never moves fast. He never attacks.
He just stands there and watches. For minutes. Sometimes for what feels like hours.
Witnesses say the worst part is not fear. It is the feeling of being studied.
Like he is waiting for something only he understands.
**[ON SCREEN: sketched composite description building up — height marker, hat, coat — like a police sketch board]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Close-up of a charcoal police-sketch style drawing board in a dim room, sketch of a tall man with a wide-brimmed hat and long coat, ruler marks showing 6 feet, moody desk lamp lighting, film grain, no readable text, 16:9

### beat-004
Most encounters happen during sleep paralysis.
You wake up, but your body refuses to move. You cannot scream. You cannot run.
Your brain is fully alert. Your muscles are still asleep.
Scientists call it a glitch between dreaming and waking. Your mind is on. Your body is off.
And in that trapped, helpless moment, he appears.
Usually in the corner. Sometimes in the doorway. Always watching.
Your heart pounds so hard you are sure it will burst.
And he does not move a single inch.
**[ON SCREEN: POV shot of a person waking in bed, room dark, figure materializing in the corner]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** First-person point of view from a bed, waking up in a dark bedroom, a tall hat-wearing shadow figure materializing in the far corner of the room, bedsheets visible at the bottom of frame, grainy low-light photography, no text, 16:9

### beat-005
Skeptics say it is all brain chemistry.
A stressed, exhausted brain, they argue, fills the darkness with a familiar shape.
Sleep paralysis hallucinations are common, they say. Millions of people get them.
The brain panics. It invents a threat. It projects a figure into the room.
Nothing supernatural. Nothing real. Just neurons misfiring in the dark.
It sounds reasonable. It sounds scientific. It sounds comforting.
There is only one problem with it.
**[ON SCREEN: calm scientific diagrams — brain scan, sleep cycle chart — then the image glitches]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** A brain scan monitor glowing in a dark laboratory, the screen glitching with a faint silhouette of a hat-wearing figure reflected in the glass, cold fluorescent light, subtle film grain, no readable text, 16:9

### beat-006
Brain chemistry should produce random hallucinations.
Spiders crawling on the ceiling. Demons with red eyes. The feeling of falling.
Your own worst fears, pulled straight from your own memories.
A scared child in Japan should see something different from a scared adult in Brazil.
Different cultures. Different fears. Different monsters.
So why do thousands of unconnected people hallucinate the exact same man?
Same height. Same hat. Same coat. Same stillness.
Random chemistry does not draw the same portrait 12,000 times.
**[ON SCREEN: rapid montage of random nightmare imagery — spiders, falling — then cutting to one identical hat figure repeated]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Split collage of chaotic nightmare imagery — spiders, falling hands, distorted faces — all torn photographs converging toward a single centered silhouette of a man in a wide-brimmed hat, dark red ambient light, heavy grain, no text, 16:9

### beat-007
Researchers who study sleep paralysis have documented the pattern for decades.
They interview patients in sleep clinics across the world.
The details stay eerily consistent across cultures that have no contact with each other.
A tall figure. A hat. A coat. Standing. Watching. Waiting.
Some researchers call it a cultural archetype, an image passed through movies and stories.
But the reports predate the internet. They predate the movies.
Some of the oldest accounts come from people who had never heard of him at all.
**[ON SCREEN: archival footage style — old case files, redacted reports stacking up]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Stack of aged case files and folders on a wooden desk in a dark archive room, one folder open showing a blurred sketch of a hat-wearing figure, single overhead lamp, dust in the air, film grain, no readable text, 16:9

### beat-008
And then there are the cases that break the pattern entirely.
Because some witnesses were never asleep at all. Not paralyzed. Not dreaming.
Wide awake. On their feet. In the middle of their normal lives.
These are the cases that skeptics cannot explain away with brain chemistry.
Case 1. A truck driver in Texas.
It happened on a lonely stretch of highway, long after midnight.
**[ON SCREEN: highway at night, dashboard camera aesthetic, title card: CASE 1]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Nighttime view from inside a truck cabin on an empty Texas highway, dashboard glow, through the windshield a tall dark figure in a wide-brimmed hat standing at the road's edge under a streetlight, cinematic, grainy, no text, 16:9

### beat-009
He had pulled over at a rest stop at 2 in the morning, wide awake, drinking coffee.
17 years on the road. He had seen everything. Nothing scared him anymore.
He stepped out of the cab to stretch his legs and clear his head.
That is when he saw him.
A man in a long coat and a wide-brimmed hat, standing between the parked trucks, staring straight at him.
Not moving. Not blinking. Just staring from about 30 feet away.
**[ON SCREEN: rest stop parking lot, rows of trucks, the figure between them]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Dark highway rest stop at 2 a.m., rows of parked semi trucks, between two trucks a tall motionless figure in a long coat and wide-brimmed hat staring toward camera, harsh sodium lights, film grain, no text, 16:9

### beat-010
The driver shouted. He yelled for the man to get away from the trucks.
The figure did not react. Did not flinch. Did not turn.
The driver took one step forward, coffee still in his hand.
And the figure was simply gone. Vanished between one blink and the next.
No footsteps. No car pulling away. No sound at all.
Just empty asphalt where a man had been standing.
The driver got back in his cab, locked the doors, and did not sleep that night.
He quit night routes 2 months later.
**[ON SCREEN: empty asphalt, a coffee cup dropped on the ground, steam rising]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Close-up of a dropped paper coffee cup on dark asphalt at night, steam rising, spilled coffee spreading, empty parking lot stretching behind it with no one there, harsh overhead light, film grain, no text, 16:9

### beat-011
Case 2. A nurse in Ohio.
She was mid-shift, fully awake, checking on her patients at 3 a.m.
12 years in the ICU. She did not believe in ghosts. She believed in medicine.
The heart monitor in room 4 started beeping. An irregular rhythm.
She walked over, calm and professional, and turned around to check the screen.
A tall man in a wide-brimmed hat stood in the doorway of room 4.
Between her and the exit. Watching the patient. Watching her.
**[ON SCREEN: hospital corridor at night, title card: CASE 2, monitor beeping waveform]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Dim hospital corridor at night, a nurse in scrubs mid-turn in the foreground, behind her a doorway framing a tall figure with a wide-brimmed hat, green glow of a heart monitor screen, grainy surveillance-camera feel, no text, 16:9

### beat-012
She froze. Her training told her to speak, to ask who he was, how he got in.
She opened her mouth. No sound came out.
She blinked hard, once, and he was gone.
The doorway was empty. The corridor beyond it was empty.
The patient in the bed never woke up. The monitor showed nothing wrong.
She searched the floor. She checked the security cameras.
The cameras showed her standing alone in an empty corridor, staring at nothing.
She never told her family. She told her supervisor. It was logged.
**[ON SCREEN: empty doorway, then the nurse's shocked face lit by monitor glow]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Empty hospital room doorway at night, pale green monitor light spilling across the floor, a nurse's shocked face half-lit in the foreground looking at the empty doorway, cinematic grain, no text, 16:9

### beat-013
And here is the part that should chill you.
Her supervisor pulled the incident logs. 2 other nurses had filed reports that same month.
Different shifts. Different nights. They had never spoken to each other about it.
All 3 described the same figure. Tall. Hat. Coat. Standing in a doorway. Gone in a blink.
None of them had ever heard of the Hat Man. None of them knew the stories.
The hospital quietly moved all 3 nurses to day shifts.
The reports were filed away and never discussed again.
**[ON SCREEN: three incident report forms, dates highlighted, the same description circled on each]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Three hospital incident report forms on a clipboard in dim light, a pen circling a hand-drawn hat shape on each form, coffee cup beside them, moody lighting, film grain, no readable text, 16:9

### beat-014
Case 3 is the one researchers quietly discuss among themselves.
A man in another country, who had never heard the stories, described him perfectly.
Hat. Coat. Height. The watching. The stillness. Every detail matched.
A researcher asked him to draw the figure from memory.
He sketched it in under a minute. Tall man. Wide-brimmed hat. Long coat.
Then the researcher showed him 20 other sketches, drawn by strangers on other continents.
He went pale. They were identical to his.
He had never seen the internet forums. He had never read a single account.
He drew the same man anyway.
**[ON SCREEN: a hand sketching the figure, then the sketch compared side by side with others — identical]**
**IMAGE PROMPT:** Close-up of a hand sketching with charcoal on paper in a dim room, the drawing showing a tall man with a wide-brimmed hat and long coat, beside it two other sketches of the identical figure, candlelight, film grain, no readable text, 16:9

### beat-015
So what is he?
A glitch in the sleeping brain? A shared nightmare passed quietly through culture?
Or something that learned, a long time ago, that the edge of sleep is the easiest door to walk through?
The skeptics have their answer. The witnesses have theirs.
But 12,000 strangers cannot all be wrong about the same hat.
Tonight, when you wake up at 3 a.m. and cannot move, check the corner of your room.
And if he is already looking back at you, do not scream. He likes that.
