# SCRIPT — "The Moon Is Rusting — And It Should Be Impossible"
# Channel: Cosmic Unknown (@CosmosUnknownTV) | Format: LONG 16:9, ~7 min
# Topic gut-check: uncommon idea (rust where rust is impossible) + broad TAM (space-curious) + fresh lens (detective story: Earth is the culprit). Validated: covered by NASA/BBC/ACS 2020-2025.

## HOOK VARIANTS (3, different categories)
### Hook A — Curiosity Gap (CHOSEN)
Spoken: "The Moon is rusting. And on an airless world with no water — that should be completely impossible."
On-screen: "THE MOON IS RUSTING"
First frame: extreme macro of rust-red oxide streaks across grey cratered lunar soil.
Why chosen: names the contradiction in one breath; spoken, on-screen text, and first frame fire the SAME claim simultaneously; opens a loop ("how?") it never answers. Stronger than B (vaguer) and C (split-frame visual divides attention).

### Hook B — Contrarian
Spoken: "Forget what you learned about rust. The Moon just broke every rule in the book."
On-screen: "RUST SHOULDN'T EXIST HERE"
First frame: grey Moon surface with one impossible red-orange rust patch.
Why not: "every rule in the book" is vaguer and less specific than the paradox itself.

### Hook C — Question / Open Loop
Spoken: "How does a rock rust when it's never touched air — or a single drop of water?"
On-screen: "NO AIR. NO WATER. RUST?"
First frame: split — rusty Earth nail beside pristine grey Moon rock.
Why not: strong question, but the split-frame visual splits the viewer's attention in the first 0.5s.
