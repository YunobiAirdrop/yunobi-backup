# Short 2 — The Hum
Judul: Thousands Hear a Hum That DOESN'T Exist #shorts #weirdfacts #foryou
Hook on-screen: A HUM NOBODY CAN FIND
Hook variants: A) "Thousands of people hear a low hum every night — and scientists can't find where it's coming from." B) "There's a sound driving people out of their own homes. No one can locate it." C) "2% of people hear it. 98% hear nothing. What is the Hum?"

### beat-001
Thousands of people hear a low hum every single night — a deep drone, like a truck idling far away. Except there's no truck.
**[ON SCREEN: bedroom window at night, person sitting up in bed looking disturbed, dark room, cinematic photo]**

### beat-002
From Bristol to Taos to Berlin, about two to four percent of people hear it. They've moved houses. Soundproofed bedrooms. Some fled their cities trying to escape it.
**[ON SCREEN: world map at night with glowing dots on cities, dark background, infographic documentary style]**

### beat-003
Theories? Industrial machines. Ocean waves. Even a rare form of tinnitus. In a few towns, a factory was blamed. But in most cases, no source was ever found.
**[ON SCREEN: industrial factory silhouette at night with smokestacks, orange glow, hazy, documentary photo]**

### beat-004
The hum has no confirmed origin — and for the people who hear it, it never stops. Follow for stranger facts.
**[ON SCREEN: close-up of an ear in darkness with faint sound wave ripples, artistic documentary photo]**
