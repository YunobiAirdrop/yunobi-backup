### BEAT 2 — restate and raise

### BEAT 3 — the killer chemistry
It starts with the volcano. Ol Doinyo Lengai — an active volcano in northern Tanzania — erupts ash loaded with sodium carbonate. Rain washes it into a shallow basin with no outlet. The brutal sun evaporates the water, and what's left behind gets more concentrated every year. Today the lake's pH sits above ten — about as alkaline as household ammonia — and in the shallows, the water can hit sixty degrees Celsius.
[ON SCREEN: Ol Doinyo Lengai volcano at dusk, dark symmetrical cone rising over the savanna plain, moody sky, documentary landscape photography]

### BEAT 4 — how the statues form
That chemistry is the same mineral ancient Egyptians used to mummify their dead. They called it natron. When birds or bats misjudge the glassy surface and crash into the lake, the alkaline water coats every feather in a crust of minerals. Photographer Nick Brandt found them lined up along the shore — eagles, swallows, bats — stiff, chalk-white, posed like museum statues. The lake didn't carve them. It embalmed them.
[ON SCREEN: macro photo of white sodium carbonate salt crystals crusting cracked red mud at the lake edge, shallow depth of field]

### BEAT 5 — the twist: flamingo nursery
Now the twist. The deadliest lake in Africa is also the world's greatest flamingo nursery. Every year, up to two and a half million lesser flamingos — most of the planet's population — fly here to breed. It is the only place they do it regularly. Why would any parent choose this? Because nothing else can survive here. The caustic water is a moat. Predators can't cross it. The lake that kills everything is the safest nursery on Earth.
[ON SCREEN: massive flock of lesser flamingos taking flight over blood-red Lake Natron, pink birds against crimson water, wildlife documentary photo]

### BEAT 6 — how they survive
The flamingos cheat. They drink from freshwater springs bubbling up through the lakebed. They eat the toxic blue-green algae that poisons everything else — and that algae is exactly what paints their feathers pink. Their legs are coated in tough, scaly skin, and they build their nests as little mud islands, keeping their chicks above the burn. Evolution didn't just tolerate this lake. It weaponized it.
[ON SCREEN: lesser flamingo chicks on mud-mound nests at the lake shore, shallow pink-reflecting water, documentary photo]

### BEAT 7 — human warning
For people, the rule is simple: don't touch it. The red crust at the edges looks like solid ground, but underneath it hides mud that can burn skin and blind eyes. Local Maasai herders give it a wide berth. Honestly — so should you.

## FAKTA YANG DIVERIFIKASI (jangan ubah angka)
- Lake Natron, Tanzania utara, di kaki gunung berapi aktif Ol Doinyo Lengai
- pH > 10 (setara amonia rumah tangga), suhu perairan dangkal s/d 60°C
- Natrium karbonat = "natron" dipakai mumifikasi Mesir kuno
- Nick Brandt mendokumentasikan burung/kelelawar terkalsifikasi di tepi danau
- s/d 2,5 juta flamingo kecil berkembang biak di sini; satu-satunya lokasi rutin
- Flamingo minum dari mata air tawar, makan alga spirulina (sumber warna pink)
