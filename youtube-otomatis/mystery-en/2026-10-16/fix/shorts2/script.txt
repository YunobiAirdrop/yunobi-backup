In the summer of 1518, a woman stepped into the street in Strasbourg and started dancing. She didn't stop. For days. Then others joined her. Dozens of people, dancing uncontrollably, day and night, until their feet bled. Some collapsed. Some died, hearts giving out mid-dance. Nobody knew why. Poisoned bread? Mass hysteria? A curse? The dancing plague lasted for weeks, then vanished as strangely as it came. Five hundred years later, science still can't fully explain it.
