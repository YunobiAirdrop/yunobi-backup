George and Jennie Sodder had ten children. That night, nine were home. The parents slept on the ground floor. Maurice, fourteen. Martha, twelve. Louis, nine. Jennie, eight. And little Betty, five — all slept upstairs. Around half past midnight, the phone rang with a wrong number. Then, near one in the morning, Jennie woke to smoke. She ran upstairs, but the stairway was already a wall of fire. George grabbed his ladder to reach the second floor. The ladder was gone. It should have been leaning against the house. It had vanished.
