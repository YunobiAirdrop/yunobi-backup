One in the morning, May twenty-ninth. The war drums start, and they do not stop. Wave after wave crashes against the walls — irregular troops first, thrown in to exhaust the defenders, then the elite Janissaries, the sultan's finest soldiers. For hours, the impossible happens: the defenders hold. The breach is plugged with living bodies. Then, catastrophe. A small postern gate in the wall — the Kerkoporta — is found unlocked, left open by mistake in the chaos. Ottoman soldiers pour through it and take the defenders from behind. At the same moment, the great cannon finally blasts a real, unpluggable breach in the outer wall, and the Janissaries charge screaming through the gap. Constantine the Eleventh tears off his purple imperial cloak so he won't be recognized — and charges into the melee with his remaining men. He is never seen alive again. By sunrise, the horse-tail standard of the sultan flies over the palace. The thousand-year wall has fallen in fifty-three days.
