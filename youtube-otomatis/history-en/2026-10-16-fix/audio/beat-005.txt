April sixth, fourteen fifty-three. The bombardment begins. Day after day, the great cannon hammers the same stretch of the Theodosian Walls. Each shot shakes the city like an earthquake. Each night, the defenders scramble out with rubble, timber, barrels of earth, even bales of wool ,  patching the cracks before dawn. It is brutal, grinding, exhausting work. And it is working. The walls are holding. Then Mehmed tries the sea. His fleet ,  hundreds of vessels ,  sails for the Golden Horn, the city's great natural harbor. And stops dead. The Byzantines have stretched a massive iron chain across the mouth of the harbor, link by link, floating on wooden pontoons. No ship can pass it. The entire Ottoman navy sits useless outside the chain while the siege drags into its second week, then its third. The walls still stand. And the young sultan is running out of time ,  because a Christian relief fleet could sail over the horizon any day now. He needs a miracle. So he invents one.
