Now the city is being squeezed from land and sea at the same time, and the bombardment never stops. The great cannon has cracked the outer wall in a dozen places. The defenders are exhausted — sleeping in their armor on the ramparts, eating whatever is left in the granaries. Inside the city, the mood turns to dread. Every night, Emperor Constantine the Eleventh walks the walls himself, speaking to his men by name, urging them to hold. On May twenty-sixth, Mehmed sends a final offer: surrender the city peacefully, and Constantine can keep his life, his riches, and rule some other territory. It is a generous deal. Constantine refuses. His reply becomes legend: the city belongs to God and to the empire, and he will not give it to anyone. He knows exactly what is coming. On the night of May twenty-eighth, the churches fill with thousands of people praying. The emperor takes communion in Hagia Sophia. Then he rides out to the walls — for the last time.
