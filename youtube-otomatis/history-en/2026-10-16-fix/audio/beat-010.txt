So why does this still matter, almost six hundred years later? Because Constantinople proved something terrifying: no wall is forever. The cannon beat the stone — and from that day forward, every fortress on Earth was obsolete. Armies stopped building taller walls and started building better guns. The age of castles ended at that breach, and the age of artillery began. The Middle Ages didn't end with a treaty or a coronation. They ended with a twenty-one-year-old pointing the biggest gun on Earth at the oldest wall on Earth — and pulling the trigger. Remember that the next time someone tells you you're too young to change the world. Mehmed the Second moved seventy warships over a mountain, broke a thousand years of stone in fifty-three days, and ended an empire before breakfast.
