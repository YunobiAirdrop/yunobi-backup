What is coming is an army of eighty thousand men, led by a boy. Mehmed the Second. Twenty-one years old. He has been sultan for only two years, and the old viziers at his court still treat him like a child playing dress-up. They are wrong. Mehmed speaks six languages — Turkish, Arabic, Persian, Greek, Latin, and Serbian. He studies Alexander the Great the way other boys study games, and he has read every account of every failed siege of Constantinople. His own father tried and failed. His grandfather tried and failed. For eight hundred years, Muslim armies have broken against those walls. Mehmed is obsessed with being the one who finally takes the city — and he has a plan neither his father nor his grandfather ever had. It starts with a cannon so enormous that when people first see the drawings, they laugh out loud.
